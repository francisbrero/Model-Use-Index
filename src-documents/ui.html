What I'd actually build

These are five different answers to “what is a dashboard for”, not five skins. They are not mutually exclusive, and picking one is the wrong exercise — the useful question is which one you land on when you open the tool.

  1. 02 The Matrix is the landing view. It is the most direct rendering of the question you started with, and it is the only view where a misallocation has a visible shape.
  2. 03 The Queue is where the value is realised. A tool that only describes spend gets looked at twice and then forgotten. One that proposes a change and takes the change gets used. Everything in the PRD exists to make these cards trustworthy.
  3. 01 The Ledger is the drill-down, not the home page. Keep it exactly as complete as your reference report — it is what you open when someone disputes a number.
  4. 04 The Trace is the credibility layer. The first time the tool says underprovisioned, you will not believe it. This is the view that settles it.
  5. 05 The Flow is not part of the tool. Build it as an export when you need to put one image in front of a budget holder.

Sequencing follows the PRD phases: 01 ships in Phase 2 because it needs only classification. 02 and 04 arrive in Phase 3 with the outcome signals. 03 comes last, once the gold set says the verdicts are worth acting on.

Two things changed in v0.2 and they touch every screen. The unit is now points of allowance rather than dollars — on seat plans the scarce thing is frontier headroom, not money, and a headroom claim can actually be checked next week by watching the limit hits stop. And the pool-balance strip under 02 is doing quiet but important work: it is the only place that answers whether delegating to Codex is the right move, because that is a transfer between ceilings rather than a saving, and the answer depends on which ceiling is binding this week.

Figures throughout are the 28 rows from your reference report, re-scored through the verdict rules in §8 of the PRD and re-expressed as share of a 30-day allowance. The session trace in 04, the pool ceilings in 02, the limit-hit counts and the unit counts in 03 are illustrative — that data does not exist yet, and whether allowance consumption is even machine-readable is Phase 0 question #3.
